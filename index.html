4. Systematic inspection checklist

A. Entry, scope and records
Confirm proprietor/person in charge, explain purpose and scope, and record who is present.Routine
Verify registration/notification details, classification, activities, trading name and ownership.Major
Verify food safety supervisor requirements and evidence of current competency where applicable.Major
Confirm required food safety program or minimum records are available, current and used in practice.Major
Review previous non-compliances, complaints, sampling results and outstanding actions.Routine
Assess Standard 3.2.2A applicability: trained food handlers, food safety supervisor and substantiation/evidence requirements.Major
B. Receiving and approved suppliers
Food is obtained from identifiable, lawful and suitable suppliers; invoices or traceability information are available.Major
Potentially hazardous food is received at safe temperature and protected from contamination.Critical
Packaging is intact; food is within use-by date; no swollen, leaking, damaged or contaminated containers.Major
Delivery vehicles, transport containers and handover procedures maintain temperature and prevent contamination.Critical
C. Storage and temperature control
Cold potentially hazardous food is maintained at 5°C or below, unless another safe control is demonstrated.Critical
Hot potentially hazardous food is maintained at 60°C or above, unless another safe control is demonstrated.Critical
Frozen food remains frozen; thawing is controlled and does not contaminate other food.Major
Raw food is separated from ready-to-eat food; food is covered, labelled and stored off the floor.Critical
Date marking, stock rotation and use-by controls are effective.Major
Probe thermometer is available, accurate, clean and sanitised between uses.Major
D. Food processing and contamination control
Preparation workflow prevents cross-contamination between raw and ready-to-eat foods.Critical
Cooking process achieves a safe outcome and staff can demonstrate how it is verified.Critical
Cooling: from 60°C to 21°C within 2 hours, then 21°C to 5°C within a further 4 hours, or an equally safe validated process.Critical
Reheating is rapid and food is not repeatedly cooled and reheated without effective control.Critical
Time as a control is documented and staff understand the applicable 2-hour/4-hour decision points.Critical
Reduced-oxygen process has validated controls for time, temperature, shelf life, pathogens and package integrity.Critical
Raw sprouts and ready-to-eat produce have suitable supplier assurance, washing and contamination controls.Major
E. Sushi and sashimi controls
Check the business’s documented process and council-approved food safety program/template. Acidified rice control must be measurable and consistently recorded.
Sushi rice recipe is standardised; vinegar/acid quantity, batch size, mixing method and holding period are controlled.Critical
Each batch is checked using a suitable calibrated pH method; records demonstrate the validated critical limit is met.Critical
Raw seafood is sourced from approved suppliers, traceable, protected and kept under effective temperature control.Critical
Separate utensils, boards, gloves/handwashing and display practices prevent contamination.Critical
Display time and disposal rules are documented and followed; leftover displayed sushi is not inappropriately retained.Major
Parasite controls and supplier assurances are considered for fish intended to be eaten raw.Major
F. Raw egg, mayonnaise and aioli
Pasteurised egg product is used where practicable, particularly for uncooked sauces and desserts.Major
Only clean, uncracked eggs are used; dirty or cracked eggs are discarded and shells do not contact food.Critical
Raw egg product is prepared in small batches using sanitised equipment and protected from cross-contamination.Critical
Product is kept at 5°C or below, date/time labelled, not topped up, and discarded within the business’s validated short shelf life.Critical
Staff understand higher risk to vulnerable people and the business avoids serving unsafe raw egg foods to these groups.Major
G. Personal hygiene and handwashing
Dedicated handwash basin is accessible, unobstructed and supplied with warm running water, soap and single-use towels.Critical
Food handlers wash hands at appropriate times and do not use food or utensil sinks for handwashing.Critical
Illness reporting and exclusion procedures cover vomiting, diarrhoea and other foodborne illness symptoms.Critical
Clothing, wounds, gloves and personal behaviour do not create contamination risk.Major
H. Cleaning, sanitising and equipment
Food-contact surfaces are clean, smooth, impervious and in good repair.Major
Cleaning and sanitising are separate, effective steps; chemical concentration/contact time or heat process is correct.Critical
Dishwasher/glasswasher operation is suitable and monitored; manual washing setup is adequate where used.Major
Cleaning cloths, brushes and chemicals are stored and used to prevent contamination.Major
Equipment is accessible for cleaning; seals, blades, slicers, ice machines and hard-to-reach areas are satisfactory.Major
I. Premises, pests, waste and utilities
Floors, walls, ceilings, fixtures, lighting and ventilation are suitable, clean and maintained.Major
No evidence of rodents, cockroaches, flies or other pests; entry points and harbourage are controlled.Critical
Waste is contained and removed without attracting pests or contaminating food areas.Major
Adequate potable water, hot water, sewage disposal and drainage are available and operating.Critical
Toilets and staff facilities are suitable and do not open directly into food preparation areas without appropriate separation.Major
J. Allergen management
Staff can accurately identify ingredients and mandatory allergens in menu items.Critical
Allergen orders are clearly communicated from customer to kitchen and through service.Critical
Cross-contact controls cover hands, benches, utensils, fryers, grills, storage and garnishes.Critical
Packaged food labels and menu claims are accurate; staff do not make unsupported “allergen-free” assurances.Critical
K. Buffet and self-service
Food is protected by covers, guards, supervision and suitable utensils.Major
Hot/cold holding or documented time control is effective and verified.Critical
Contaminated or returned food is discarded and serving utensils are replaced appropriately.Critical
L. Closing discussion and enforcement decision
Discuss findings with proprietor/person in charge and verify their understanding.Routine
For each non-compliance, record evidence, risk, legislative basis, required action and due date.Major
Apply proportionate enforcement in accordance with council policy, procedural fairness and immediate public-health risk.Critical
Arrange follow-up, sampling, referral, seizure/disposal or escalation where required.Major

Reference notes

This is a practical prompt, not a substitute for the Food Act 1984 (Vic), Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code, council procedures, authorised-officer powers, current Department of Health guidance or the premises-specific food safety program.
  • Potentially hazardous food generally requires temperature control at 5°C or below or 60°C or above, unless another safe process is demonstrated.
  • Standard 3.2.2A may require a food safety supervisor, food-handler training and evidence that critical controls are being managed.
  • Raw egg foods and sushi require close examination of documented recipes, time/temperature controls, verification records and staff practice.
